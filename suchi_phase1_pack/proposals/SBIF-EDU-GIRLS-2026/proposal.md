Diksha Foundation proposes the Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) to address the critical gaps in foundational learning and girls' education across Bihar, directly impacting out-of-school girls and those at risk of dropping out [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Our initiative will serve beneficiaries aged 6-14 across three KHEL centers: Sarairanjan, Patna, and Bihta [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. This intervention directly responds to the urgent need for improved educational outcomes in a state grappling with significant challenges in literacy, numeracy, and girls' access to quality education.

Bihar faces persistent educational disparities, with enrollment rates lagging behind national averages, particularly for girls in marginalized communities [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. {{MISSING: Dropout rates}} are exacerbated by socioeconomic factors, limited access to resources, and inadequate infrastructure, creating a significant learning crisis that demands immediate attention [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. While government initiatives and existing programs strive to address these issues, a critical gap remains in providing targeted, holistic support that caters to the unique needs of out-of-school girls and those at risk of dropping out, especially in foundational literacy, numeracy, and socio-emotional learning [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

The Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) directly addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive, integrated learning environment that focuses on foundational literacy and numeracy skills, alongside socio-emotional learning and life skills development [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Our approach aligns with the goals of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, which emphasizes foundational learning as a cornerstone of quality education and equitable access for all children, particularly girls [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. By establishing and strengthening our three KHEL centers in Sarairanjan, Patna, and Bihta, we aim to create safe, supportive spaces where girls can thrive academically, develop essential life skills, and build confidence to pursue their education and future aspirations [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

Diksha Foundation is a registered society (Reg. S/RS/SW/0019/2) committed to empowering marginalized communities through education and skill development.

## Proposed Intervention

The Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) will empower out-of-school girls and girls at risk of dropout across Bihar by providing foundational literacy and numeracy skills, social-emotional learning, and life skills development. Our team will directly serve girls aged 6-14 across our 3 KHEL centers in Sarairanjan, Patna, and Bihta [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1], addressing the critical need for foundational learning and girls' education in alignment with SBI Foundation's priorities [citation:proposal_001:chunk_2].

Our Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) builds upon Diksha Foundation's established KHEL (Knowledge Hub for Education and Learning) model, which operates through a Hub & Spoke structure [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Currently, our 3 KHEL centers in Patna, Bihta, and Sarairanjan serve 511 learners [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. The ILM program will specifically target out-of-school girls and girls at risk of dropout, providing them with access to quality education and a supportive learning environment. We will leverage our existing infrastructure and expertise to deliver a comprehensive program that addresses the unique needs of these girls, focusing on foundational literacy and numeracy, digital literacy, sports, and social-emotional learning (SEE Learning).

The ILM curriculum will integrate foundational literacy and numeracy with social-emotional learning (SEL) and life skills, fostering holistic development. We will adapt the SEE Learning framework to the local context, training our 8 Fellow Teachers in its principles and practices [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. This approach will equip the girls with the skills and confidence to succeed in education, employment, and life, contributing to positive social change within their communities, aligning with Diksha Foundation's mission and SBI Foundation's impact goals [citation:proposal_001:chunk_2]. The program will be delivered by our dedicated team including 3 Center Coordinators, 8 Fellow Teachers, a Computer Instructor, an IT volunteer, volunteer facilitators, a Project Lead, an External Sports Trainer, and a part-time Sports Coach [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

## Problem Statement

Bihar faces significant challenges in ensuring foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) for all children, particularly girls aged 6-14 who are out of school or at risk of dropping out. Our Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) directly addresses this critical gap in Patna, Samastipur, and Bihta, aligning with national and state priorities for foundational learning and girls' education.

Despite progress in enrollment, significant learning deficits persist in Bihar. ASER 2022 data indicates that a substantial percentage of children in early grades lack basic reading and math skills [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. This is further compounded by socioeconomic factors and gender disparities, with girls often facing greater barriers to accessing quality education and completing their schooling [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. These challenges are particularly acute in districts like Patna, Samastipur, Gaya, and Muzaffarpur, where poverty and social inequalities limit educational opportunities [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated these issues, leading to learning losses and increased dropout rates, especially among vulnerable populations [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

Existing interventions often fall short of addressing the holistic needs of out-of-school girls and those at risk of dropping out. While government initiatives and NGOs are working to improve access to education, there remains a gap in providing targeted support that addresses both academic and socio-emotional needs [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Many programs lack the individualized attention, remedial support, and life skills training necessary to help these girls catch up academically, build confidence, and stay in school [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Our ILM program is designed to fill this gap by providing a comprehensive learning environment that combines foundational literacy and numeracy instruction with social-emotional learning (SEL) and life skills development, directly supporting the goals of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

Diksha Foundation's ILM program will serve 3 KHEL centers in Patna, Bihta, and Sarairanjan, reaching 511 learners. By focusing on personalized learning, SEL, and community engagement, we aim to empower these girls to overcome barriers to education, achieve their full potential, and contribute to a more equitable and prosperous Bihar [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1].

### Implementation Plan

The Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) will provide comprehensive support to out-of-school girls and girls at risk of dropout across our 3 KHEL centers, reaching 511 learners in Patna, Bihta, and Sarairanjan [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. Our integrated approach combines academic support, digital literacy, sports, and social-emotional learning (SEL) to foster holistic development and ensure these girls not only re-enter formal education but also thrive [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1].

Our program's weekly schedule is designed to provide a balanced and engaging learning experience. Academic sessions focus on foundational literacy and numeracy, using a supplementary education model aligned with the ASER framework [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. We provide workbooks and teaching-learning materials to reinforce concepts [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. Digital literacy sessions, held 5 times per week, equip girls with essential computer skills using Khan Academy and internal digital literacy modules [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. The current device ratio is approximately 1:3 (to be confirmed with latest inventory). Sports and physical activity are integrated 3 times per week, with Fellow Teachers providing coaching and guidance [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. We provide basic sports equipment such as balls and mats, ensuring an inclusive environment for all genders [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. SEL sessions, also held 3 times per week, are delivered by our trained Fellow Teachers using an age-appropriate curriculum [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1].

Community engagement is a vital component of our implementation plan. We will organize quarterly community open days and monthly parent-teacher meetings to foster strong relationships with families and the wider community (dates to be finalized) [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1]. These events will provide opportunities to share progress, address concerns, and build a supportive ecosystem for the girls [citation:proposal_005:chunk_1].

The implementation timeline for an initial year is structured around quarterly milestones. In Q1, we will focus on enrollment, baseline assessments, and staff training. Q2 will emphasize curriculum implementation and the establishment of regular academic, digital literacy, sports, and SEL sessions. Q3 will involve mid-term assessments, community engagement events, and program adjustments based on data. Finally, Q4 will be dedicated to end-of-year evaluations, impact reporting, and planning for the subsequent year.

We will employ a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative data on attendance and academic performance with qualitative insights into social-emotional learning and program participation [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. This comprehensive monitoring and evaluation (M&E) framework will allow us to continuously improve the program and demonstrate its impact to SBI Foundation.

Our M&E Officer will oversee the entire process, ensuring data quality and timely reporting [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. ### Sustainability and Replication

The Integrated Learning Mission (ILM) will ensure lasting impact by empowering local youth, leveraging existing infrastructure, fostering community ownership, and diversifying our funding base. Our approach ensures that the 511 learners across our 3 KHEL centers in Patna, Bihta, and Sarairanjan continue to benefit long after the grant period [citation:proposal_001:chunk_3].

Our youth-led sustainability model centers on cultivating Young Leaders from within the communities we serve. We will train 9 Young Leaders as mediators and coaches, equipping them with the skills to progressively lead sessions independently [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. These Young Leaders will undergo a structured training program covering mediation techniques, sports coaching, and life skills facilitation. Our target is to retain at least 75% of these trained Young Leaders in active roles within the KHEL centers for at least one year post-grant, ensuring continuity of program delivery and local ownership.

The ILM program leverages existing infrastructure to minimize costs and maximize sustainability. No new facilities require construction, and equipment is reusable and low-cost [citation:proposal_001:chunk_1]. Our 3 KHEL centers in Patna, Bihta, and Sarairanjan provide safe and accessible learning spaces [citation:proposal_001:chunk_3]. These centers will continue to operate as community hubs, offering educational and recreational activities beyond the grant period.

Community ownership is fostered through active parent engagement and community events. We will organize quarterly community open days at each KHEL center to showcase student progress, promote program awareness, and solicit feedback [citation:proposal_001:chunk11]. By linking education with sports, we address the "study vs. play" cultural barrier, demonstrating how both contribute to a child's holistic development. This approach resonates with families' values and encourages their sustained involvement in their children's education.

To ensure long-term financial sustainability, we are committed to diversifying our funding base. Currently, Diksha Foundation receives support from funders such as the Azim Premji Foundation and Feeding India [citation:proposal_001:chunk_10]. We will actively pursue additional funding opportunities from corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiatives, philanthropic organizations, and government grants to support the continued operation and expansion of the ILM program.
