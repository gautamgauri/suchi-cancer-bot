## When to See a Doctor

Any new breast lump or concerning change should be evaluated immediately by a doctor. Do not delay — early detection significantly improves outcomes.

### Warning signs to get checked:
- A new lump or thickening in the breast or underarm area
- Change in breast size or shape
- Dimpling or puckering of the skin
- Nipple discharge (especially if bloody)
- Nipple turning inward (retraction)
- Redness, scaling, or thickening of the nipple or breast skin
- Persistent breast pain that does not go away

## Diagnosis Pathway

### Step 1: Clinical Examination
- Doctor performs a physical examination of the breast and underarm area
- Medical history is taken, including family history of cancer

### Step 2: Imaging Tests
- **Mammogram:** X-ray of the breast — primary imaging tool for breast cancer detection
- **Breast ultrasound:** Often used alongside mammogram, especially for dense breast tissue
- **MRI:** May be recommended in certain cases (high-risk patients, or to assess extent of disease)

### Step 3: Biopsy
- **Core needle biopsy:** Most common method — a small tissue sample is taken from the lump using a needle
- The tissue sample is sent to the pathology lab for examination
- Pathology report will include: tumor type, grade, and receptor status

### Step 4: Pathology Report — Key Information
The pathology report is critical for treatment planning. Key details include:
- **Tumor type:** Most common is invasive ductal carcinoma
- **Tumor size and grade:** Indicates how aggressive the cancer is
- **Receptor status:**
  - **ER (Estrogen Receptor):** Positive or negative
  - **PR (Progesterone Receptor):** Positive or negative
  - **HER2 (Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2):** Positive or negative
- These markers determine which treatments will be most effective

### Step 5: Staging
- Additional imaging (CT scan, bone scan, PET-CT) may be done to check if cancer has spread
- Staging determines the extent of the disease (Stages I through IV)
- Stage determines the treatment approach

## Treatment Options

According to WHO, breast cancer treatment usually combines multiple approaches:

### Surgery
- **Lumpectomy (breast-conserving surgery):** Removes the tumor and a margin of surrounding tissue
- **Mastectomy:** Removes the entire breast
- **Sentinel lymph node biopsy:** Checks if cancer has spread to nearby lymph nodes

### Radiation Therapy
- Often given after lumpectomy to reduce risk of recurrence
- May also be given after mastectomy in certain cases
- Typically 3-6 weeks of daily sessions

### Systemic Therapy (Medications)
Treatment depends on the receptor status of the tumor:
- **Chemotherapy:** Used for many breast cancers, especially aggressive types
- **Hormone therapy (endocrine therapy):** For ER/PR positive tumors — includes tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors
- **Targeted therapy:** For HER2 positive tumors — includes trastuzumab (Herceptin)
- Treatment regimens are individualized based on tumor characteristics and stage

## Questions Caregivers Should Ask the Medical Team

1. What is the cancer subtype (ER/PR/HER2 status)?
2. What stage is the cancer?
3. What are the treatment options, and what does the recommended plan look like?
4. What are the expected side effects of each treatment?
5. How long will the treatment take?
6. What tests will be done before and during treatment?
7. What symptoms should we watch for and report immediately?
8. Are there financial assistance options available?
9. Are there clinical trials available?
10. What follow-up care will be needed after treatment?

## Practical Preparation for Each Appointment

1. **Bring identification** (Aadhaar) and PM-JAY/ABHA card if available
2. **Bring all previous medical records** — reports, scans, pathology results, treatment summaries
3. **Prepare a list of questions** to ask the doctor
4. **Have someone accompany you** — a family member or friend for support
5. **Bring current medication list** with dosages
6. **Take notes** during the consultation or record the discussion (with doctor's permission)

## Financial Assistance for Breast Cancer Treatment

- **PM-JAY (Ayushman Bharat):** Covers breast cancer surgery, chemotherapy, radiation (up to Rs. 5 lakh/year). Call 14555 for eligibility.
- **Hospital social work department:** Can help navigate schemes and NGO support
- **Indian Cancer Society:** 1800-22-1951 (toll-free) — financial assistance
- **Mahavir Cancer Sansthan (Patna):** Mahavir Mandir Trust provides free treatment for children and Rs. 10,000 aid for poor adult patients

## Emergency Warning Signs During Treatment

Seek immediate medical attention (call 112 or 108) if you experience:
- Fever above 100.4°F (38°C) during chemotherapy — this is an emergency
- Severe chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Heavy or uncontrolled bleeding
- Severe allergic reaction (swelling, difficulty breathing, rash)
- Confusion or sudden weakness
