Background and Problem Statement and Proposed Solution
Bihar faces significant challenges in education and youth development that hinder the potential of its young population. The state ranks among the lowest on India’s Human Development Index, with a literacy rate below the national average and stark gender disparities-adult women are twice as likely to be illiterate as men. Enrollment rates, especially for girls, lag behind national averages, and dropout rates increase significantly at the secondary level. These challenges are particularly acute in rural districts where access to quality education and resources is limited. Bihar’s youth bulge-28% of the population aged 15–29, represents both a massive opportunity and an urgent challenge.
Bihar also faces a persistent “study vs play” trade-off, especially for adolescent girls: families often view sport as a distraction rather than a development pathway. The local adage “If you study you will live like a king, but if you play sport you will spoil your life” reflects deep-seated beliefs that reduce sustained participation and limit safe spaces for confidence-building and peer leadership. A study of adolescents in Patna revealed a depression prevalence of 49.2%, driven by academic pressure and fear of failure. With only 32% of Bihar schools having trained physical education teachers and many rural schools lacking proper playgrounds, opportunities for structured physical development are virtually absent.
Diksha is not starting from zero: over the last 2–3 years, our team has run regular football activities with students across KHEL centres as part of broader skill-development and engagement work. What has been missing is a structured pedagogy, a youth leadership pipeline, and a consistent measurement framework. Gully Goal addresses that gap by formalizing football as a deliberate capability-building pathway-integrated with girls’ empowerment through football 3’s proven methodology of structured play, facilitated reflection, and inclusive scoring.
Diksha Foundation currently operates two complementary programs in Bihar: KHEL (Knowledge Hub for Education and Learning), serving ~511 learners across three centres with holistic education including digital literacy, academic support, and SEE Learning; and Empowering Futures, a partner-school program reaching ~260 adolescent girls with leadership and life-skills programming. Both programs are funded by existing partners including Azim Premji Foundation, Feeding India, Ruban Hospitals, BP Singh & Shakuntla Devi Foundation, Every.org, and Benevity Causes. Gully Goal is designed as the sports layer that sits across both programs, creating a unified football-for-development pathway for all ~771 learners.
Having run football regularly for 2–3 years, the program is ready for its next step: structured pedagogy, youth leadership, and measurement-so football becomes a deliberate development pathway for KHEL learners and Empowering Futures girls alike. Gully Goal aligns directly with ESA’s themes of empowering girls, fostering leadership, and strengthening grassroots sports. The program also supports SDG 4 (Quality Education), SDG 5 (Gender Equality), and SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being).


Objectives of the Project
Gully Goal aims to transform Diksha Foundation’s existing football practice into a structured, measurable football-for-development program serving ~771 learners across KHEL centres and the Empowering Futures partner-school stream over a 12-month period. The specific objectives are:
1. Establish Structured Football-for-Development Delivery: Implement the football3 methodology across all 3 KHEL centres and Empowering Futures partner locations, delivering 3 mixed football3 sessions/week and 1 girls-first session/week with trained mediators facilitating “three halves” sessions (pre-game agreements, gameplay, and post-game reflection).
2. football3 is a fair play football methodology that is used by over 70 organisations from the Common Goal Community on all continents. Across the globe, players meet, form teams, agree on rules and play football. In football3, dialogue and fair play are integral to the game.football3 can be played by anyone, anywhere and it can be used to address any social topic. There are no referees. Instead, mediators facilitate discussions between the two teams and monitor the match. Named after its ‘three halves’ – a pre-match discussion, football game, and post-match discussion – football3 incorporates key life lessons into every match. Ideally played in mixed-gender teams, players collectively decide on the rules before the game. Following the match, they reflect on their behaviour and the behaviour of their opponents, with points awarded for goals as well as for fair play.
3. Promote Physical Activity, Life Skills, and Girls’ Participation through Football3: Ensure that at least 80% of the sports cohort demonstrates improvement in football skills and conflict-resolution/teamwork behaviours, measured through a skills rubric plus a football3 fair-play/reflection rubric at baseline, midline (Month 6), and endline (Month 12). Girls’ participation will be strengthened through girls-first cohorts and leadership roles in facilitation and reflection circles. This structured approach builds on our existing 2–3 years of football practice and upgrades it into a measurable capability-building pathway.
4. Achieve and Sustain Girls’ Participation at 50% or Above: Ensure that at least 50% of active football participants across centres and partner locations are girls, supported by girls-first sessions, girls-only safe spaces, and leadership integration.
5. Strengthen Education Engagement through “No School, No Football”: Improve attendance consistency of football participants in their respective learning programmes (KHEL centre routines or Empowering Futures school engagement) by at least 10 percentage points from Month 1 baseline, reinforcing sport as an incentive for educational commitment.
6. Build a Youth Leadership Pipeline: Train and retain 4 Young Leaders (aged 18–27, 3 per centre) as football3 mediators/coaches through monthly training touchpoints, with at least 70% retained by project end. Young Leaders will progressively lead sessions, creating a sustainable local coaching cadre.
7. Extend Structured Capability-Building to Empowering Futures Girls: Ensure that ~260 girls engaged through Empowering Futures access structured sport-linked life skills and leadership practice (girls-first football cohorts, reflection circles, and leadership roles), with participation and agency tracked through simple tools aligned to the 10 Core Capabilities framework.
8. Build Community Ownership and Legitimacy: Complete at least 12 points-for-service community actions (4 per centre per year) and conduct quarterly inter-centre festivals/leagues, building family and community support for sport as a development pathway.

Main Activities and Program Implementation Details
Football-for-Development (football3)
Gully Goal implements football-for-development using the football3 methodology, designed for low-resource settings and strong life-skills outcomes. Developed originally in Colombia to address community violence, football3 has been adopted globally by street football world and is recognized by UNESCO as a proven sport-for-development approach. Sessions follow a “three halves” structure: 
(1) a pre-game circle where participants agree on rules and inclusion norms, 
(2) gameplay on small pitches (40×20m, playable on any flat ground), and 
(3) a facilitator-led reflection (“third half”) where teams discuss fairness, teamwork, conflict moments, and assign fair-play points. 
This model uses a trained mediator rather than a referee, making delivery feasible in Bihar’s context where trained sports officials are scarce, and reinforcing dialogue-based conflict resolution.
Frequency & Participation: Football3 activities will be delivered across all 3 KHEL centres and Empowering Futures partner locations through 3 football3 sessions/week for the mixed sports cohort, plus 1 girls-first session/week (girls-only or girls-priority depending on local comfort and safety). Teams will be mixed-gender during regular sessions, with inclusion scoring where fair-play points are weighted alongside goals.
Centre-Based Delivery (KHEL Learners): 511 KHEL learners access football3 sessions at their respective centres or in nearby sports grounds/turfs as part of their regular weekly schedule, complementing the academic, digital, and SEE Learning activities funded by other partners.
Partner-Linked Delivery (Empowering Futures): ~260 Empowering Futures girls access girls-first sessions and reflection circles at sports grounds/turfs  or through coordinated centre visits, enabling structured sport-for-development without requiring daily centre attendance. EF girls participate in the same football3 methodology so that Gully Goal’s benefits reach beyond centre-based learners.
Education Linkage (“No School, No Football”): Participation in football sessions is linked to consistent attendance in respective learning programmes—KHEL centre routines or Empowering Futures school engagement. This is communicated through parent meetings and reinforced through attendance tracking, directly addressing Bihar’s “study vs play” barrier by positioning sport as a reward for educational commitment.
Young Leader Pipeline (18–27): Each centre will identify and train 4 local youth  as football3 mediators/coaches. Young Leaders receive monthly training touchpoints covering mediation skills, child protection, first aid, and progressive session-leading. They earn stipends of ₹6000/month, creating local employment and a sustainable coaching cadre. This mirrors successful models from the Oscar Foundation (Mumbai) and MYSA (Kenya).
Community Ownership (Points-for-Service): Each month, participants complete a points-for-service community action (e.g., cleanliness drive, health awareness activity, or tree plantation). Completion contributes to recognition/league points and builds legitimacy with families and local stakeholders, addressing scepticism about sport’s value.
Inter-Centre Festivals & Leagues: Quarterly inter-centre events bring together learners from all KHEL centres and Empowering Futures for competitive football3 festivals with mixed-gender teams, fair-play awards, and community celebration. These events are open to families and serve as visibility moments for the program and Reliance Foundation support.
Girls’ Leadership Integration
Gully Goal’s girls-first cohorts are designed to build agency and leadership through sport. Girls who demonstrate growth are given facilitation and mediation roles within football3 sessions—leading pre-game circles, moderating post-game reflections, and mentoring younger participants. Selected girls from Empowering Futures also join leadership/reflection circles and take facilitation roles, ensuring continuity between empowerment programming and sport.

Sustainability
Gully Goal is designed for sustainability from day one. The program’s cost structure is deliberately low-football3 requires minimal equipment, no formal pitches, and no professional referees-making it one of the most cost-effective sport-for-development methodologies available.
Youth-Led Sustainability: The Young Leader pipeline is the core sustainability mechanism: local youth trained as football3 mediators/coaches progressively take ownership of delivery, reducing dependence on external trainers. By Month 12, Young Leaders will be independently leading sessions, creating a self-sustaining coaching cadre that continues beyond the grant.
Existing Infrastructure: Gully Goal layers onto Diksha’s existing KHEL centres and Empowering Futures partner-school network—no new facilities need to be built. Equipment (balls, cones, bibs) is reusable and low-cost. Community and school partnerships provide playing spaces at zero venue cost.
Community Ownership: Points-for-service activities and inter-centre festivals build family and community investment in the program. The “No School, No Football” linkage positions sport within the education framework families already value, ensuring continued support. Girls-first cohorts build peer leadership networks that sustain participation beyond formal programming.
Diversified Funding Base: Diksha Foundation’s education and development work is already supported by multiple funders (Azim Premji Foundation, Feeding India, Ruban Hospitals, BP Singh & Shakuntla Devi Foundation, Every.org, Benevity Causes). If Gully Goal demonstrates impact, the Foundation will seek to continue the sports component through CSR partnerships (leveraging CSR-1 registration), government alignment with Bihar Khel Niti and Mukhyamantri Khel Vikas Yojana, and international sport-for-development grants.
Institutional Learning: Diksha’s centralized MIS, Holistic Student Dashboard, and documentation practices ensure that Gully Goal’s model, learnings, and evidence are captured for replication and scale. The capability framework alignment strengthens the case for continued funding from development-oriented donors.
